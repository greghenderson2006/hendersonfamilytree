Residence of A. D. Henderson

Suffern, New York

History

After spending several summers in Suffern, New York, Alexander Dawson Henderson Sr., and his wife, Ella Brown Henderson, decided to make the community their permanent home. In 1906, Henderson purchased a 14-acre farm from Charles Trumper in Suffern Park and hired architect William Hoar to design a Georgian-style residence on a hill overlooking the Nyack Turnpike (present-day Route 59).

Over time, the Hendersons developed the property into an impressive country estate extending along both sides of Campbell Avenue near its intersection with the Nyack Turnpike. The grounds included a working farm, a large vegetable garden, a greenhouse, a swimming pool, and a lake stocked with fish. The family also kept a cow, while a butler and household cook attended to their domestic needs.

Henderson Sr. built a cottage on the property for his son, Alexander Henderson Jr., and his wife, Mary. The cottage stood across the Campbell road from Henderson Sr.’s two-story home at 173 South Monsey Road. Alexander Jr. and Mary lived there with their children, Alex and Mary-Ella, from 1922 to 1929. The children’s German nanny lived upstairs, and the household also employed a cook.

A postcard was made of their Suffern house looking at the residence from the bottom of the driveway. The printing at the top of the postcard reads: “Residence of Mr. A. D. Henderson, Suffern, N.Y.” Source: New York Heritage digital collections, Suffern, New York.

The residence stood for 35 years before a fire broke out on November 1, 1941, while workers were in the process of demolishing the structure. Firefighters from the Suffern and Tallman fire departments responded and prevented the partially demolished building from being completely destroyed by the flames.

On September 14, 1944, J. Le Roy Sherwood of Suffern, New York, purchased the 18-acre property.
